1 · What actually leaves with him

The closing binder transferred the assets. It transferred none of the operating knowledge. Pricing that was never written down. Referral relationships that belonged to a person, not a company. Equipment with field modifications that exist on no drawing. The reason the Thursday route starts on the west side.

The industry's own guidance is blunt about the clock: seller value decays rapidly after month three, and the standard recommendation is to extract and document institutional knowledge inside the first 90 days. The standard method is a shared drive and some recorded videos — which is why it mostly does not happen. The seller note assumes the transfer occurs; nobody measures whether it did.

“If you'd called me in 2021 and asked what a new owner needs to know, I'd have talked for twenty minutes about equipment — totally useless, that's in the manuals. Nobody asked me the thing that mattered: the facilities manager and I had a deal about after-hours calls that wasn't in any contract. My buyer billed them overtime and lost the account in five months. That's $180,000 a year that walked because nobody knew to ask one question.” Master HVAC technician, 31 years, sold a 14-truck shop in 2021 — advisory persona, Understudy strategy review

2 · What we do

Twenty-two minute recorded conversations with the outgoing owner, twice a week, for the length of the transition period. An agent runs the session against a ranked list of the knowledge gaps that carry the most revenue exposure. Everything captured is confirmed by the owner themselves, carries the session and timecode it came from, and lands in a prompt book the new owner and their crew query out loud — in the outgoing owner's own recorded voice.

3 · What you get, every week

Not a wiki you have to maintain. A ranked list that shortens, and an agenda for the next twenty-two minutes of his time:

  • 01Continuity coverage, weighted by revenue exposure — not by how many documents exist.
  • 02Revenue still dependent on one man's memory, in dollars, updated after every session.
  • 03The next 22-minute agenda, built from the gaps that expose the most money — not from a generic checklist.
  • 04Every fact sourced, with the session, the timecode, and Bud's own confirmation. Nothing enters the book unconfirmed.
  • 05Answers at the truck from week one — your crew asks out loud and hears Bud's recorded answer, before he has finished leaving.

Judge us on the third line. Anyone can record a retiring owner for twenty-two minutes. The question is whether those twenty-two minutes were spent on the $412,000 of handshake pricing or on the boiler manual you already have in a drawer.

5 · What we will not do

Fixed limits, stated before you ask

We record the outgoing owner with their signed consent, they can end any session, and they can revoke any recording for 30 days after it is made. We pay them for their time, because unpaid participation is not participation.

We capture how the business runs. We do not capture opinions about named employees, health, family, or personal finances — the outgoing owner is frequently still a creditor under the seller note and sometimes still an employee, and this record will never become a performance file on anyone.

We do not score people. We score a business's knowledge coverage. We do not sell borrower data, and the prompt book belongs to the business, not to us — it transfers again if the business is sold again.

6 · Honest limitations

  • —We have not proven the engine yet. Our claim is that ranked elicitation recovers more consequential knowledge per minute than a competent generic interview. That is under formal test with a published pass mark. If it fails the test, we will say so and we will not sell this.
  • —The recording mechanic is not novel. Consumer products already phone elderly people with an AI interviewer and play back the original voice. What we claim is different is which questions get asked, and in what order.
  • —If Bud refuses, we cannot deliver coverage. We have no data on how often owners refuse — nobody does. You would find out in week one, not week nine.
  • —Coverage is a proxy. It measures whether knowledge transferred, not whether you can operate. A leading indicator, not a verdict.

What we are asking for

  1. 1Forty minutes with you to build the first gap list from your own worry list — the accounts you already lie awake about.
  2. 2One introduction to Bud, made by you, not by us. He has to hear it from the person who bought his life's work.
  3. 3Two sessions before you decide. If the second session does not surface something you did not know and could not have found in a drawer, you owe us nothing and we stop.

Appendix B · What we can and cannot tell your lender

Correction to an earlier version of this document

We previously claimed this reduces lender defaults. We withdraw that claim.

An earlier draft of this proposal was addressed to an SBA lender and argued that acquisition-loan defaults cluster in the months after the seller leaves. We checked. Business-acquisition loans have the lowest default rate of any SBA 7(a) purpose — about 0.71%, roughly three times lower than new-business loans — and the median time to default is 4.1 years, not months six through eighteen.

So the honest position is this: your lender does not have a default problem that we solve, and we will not tell them otherwise to help you close. If your lender asks what we are, the accurate answer is that we are a transition instrument you chose, not a credit control. We would rather lose the referral than earn it on a number that does not hold.

What we would say to them, and can support: seller value decays rapidly after month three, the transition period is the only window in which this knowledge is recoverable, and this engagement produces a dated, sourced record that the business keeps.
