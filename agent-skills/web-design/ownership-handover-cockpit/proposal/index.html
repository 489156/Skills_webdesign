A 90-day instrument that captures the outgoing owner's operating knowledge while they are still reachable, and gives your credit file one page a month showing whether the transition is actually on track.

1 · The window we are talking about

Acquisition loans do not usually fail at underwriting. They fail in the months after the previous owner stops answering the phone — after the transition period ends and before the new owner has learned what the business actually runs on. Pricing that was never written down. Referral relationships that belonged to a person, not a company. Equipment with field modifications that exist on no drawing.

Nothing in the closing binder transfers any of it. The seller note assumes it happens; the transition period assumes it happens; nobody instruments whether it did. Your file goes quiet between the close and the first missed payment.

“My defaults cluster at months 6 through 18 — after the seller leaves and before the buyer has learned the business. If you sold that, I'd put you in front of every borrower I have, and I'd do it because it protects my book.” Stated in our review by the credit persona this proposal is written for

2 · What we do

Twenty-two minute recorded conversations with the outgoing owner, twice a week, for the length of the transition period. An agent runs the session against a ranked list of the knowledge gaps that carry the most revenue exposure. Everything captured is confirmed by the owner themselves, carries the session and timecode it came from, and lands in a prompt book the new owner and their crew query out loud — in the outgoing owner's own recorded voice.

3 · What lands in your credit file

Not a login. A one-page monthly report per borrower, in the format your analysts already read:

  • 01Continuity coverage, weighted by revenue exposure — not by how many documents exist.
  • 02Revenue still dependent on one person's memory, in dollars.
  • 03Sessions completed against sessions owed under the seller note, and days remaining.
  • 04Open critical gaps by domain — customers and pricing, site knowledge, relationships, collections.
  • 05An exception flag when an owner disengages, which is the earliest signal you can get that a transition is failing.

That last line is the one we would ask you to judge us on. An owner who stops taking the session call is a leading indicator, and today it is invisible to you until it shows up in cash flow.

4 · Pilot structure

Borrowers
12 acquisition loans closing in the next two quarters, $1M–$6M enterprise value, with a transition period of 60 days or more
Fee
Paid by the borrower from closing proceeds — $9,000–$28,000 by deal size, typical $14,500. No cost to the institution during the pilot.
Your commitment
Introduce us at term-sheet stage. Nothing added to your closing checklist, no covenant to police.
Our commitment
Monthly report per borrower, delivered in your file format, for eight quarters — including for the deals that go badly
Measured against
Coverage at day 90, owner participation rate, and a comparison of months 6–18 performance to your matched historical cohort
Exit
You can end the pilot at any point. Reports for borrowers already enrolled continue regardless.

5 · What we will not do

Fixed limits, stated before you ask

We record the outgoing owner with their signed consent, they can end any session, and they can revoke any recording for 30 days after it is made. We pay them for their time, because unpaid participation is not participation.

We capture how the business runs. We do not capture opinions about named employees, health, family, or personal finances — the outgoing owner is frequently still a creditor under the seller note and sometimes still an employee, and this record will never become a performance file on anyone.

We do not score people. We score a business's knowledge coverage. We do not sell borrower data, and the prompt book belongs to the business, not to us — it transfers again if the business is sold again.

6 · Honest limitations

  • —We have no default-rate evidence yet. That is what the pilot is for. Any figure we quoted you before the pilot would be a model, and we will not present a model as a result.
  • —If an owner refuses to participate, we cannot deliver coverage, and we will tell you that in the first monthly report rather than the fourth.
  • —Coverage is a proxy. It measures whether knowledge transferred, not whether the buyer can operate. It is a leading indicator, not a verdict.

What we are asking for

  1. 1A 40-minute call with you and one credit analyst, to put the monthly report into your format before we write a line of it.
  2. 2Two borrowers currently at term sheet, with transition periods of 60 days or more. Two, not twelve — we would rather earn the other ten.
  3. 3Your matched historical cohort for months 6–18, so the comparison is yours and not ours.

Appendix A · The page the buyer sees

For Cal Whitmore · buyer · day 34 of 90

You have 54 days of access to the only person who knows how this business actually works.

Right now $672,000 of your annual revenue depends on things that live in one man's memory: fourteen commercial accounts priced off a handshake, four general contractors who send work because of him personally, and a bypass he installed in 2009 that appears on no drawing.

Understudy runs two 22-minute calls a week with him, prioritised by what each gap costs you if it's lost. Coverage today is 61%. Target is 85% by day 90. Your crew can query the answers out loud from the truck, in his voice, starting this week.

$14,500, paid from closing. $590/month afterwards, when the book becomes how the business answers itself. Compare that to one commercial account.

Appendix B · The page the outgoing owner sees first

For Bud Kessler · 31 years · before any question about boilers

Before we ask you anything, here is what the buyer has put in writing.

  • —The name stays on the trucks.
  • —All eleven people keep their jobs and their pay for at least twelve months.
  • —Your long-standing customers keep their pricing through the next renewal cycle.

Then, what we are asking of you: twenty-two minutes, twice a week, on the phone. You talk, we listen. You can stop any call, delete any answer, and take back any recording for thirty days. We will not ask you about your people's personal lives, your health, or your money. You are paid for your time.

What you get: thirty-one years of knowing how to do this ends up written down, with your name on it, instead of leaving with you.
